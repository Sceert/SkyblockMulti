## Issues and follow-up

### Mod Menu layout does not fit automatic GUI scale

The current configuration screen exceeds the available height when Minecraft uses automatic GUI scale. Lower controls are clipped. Setting GUI scale to 2 makes the screen usable.

Expected follow-up:

- Make every configuration view responsive to the available screen dimensions.
- Avoid depending on a specific GUI scale.
- Keep controls reachable on smaller window sizes and automatic scale.

### Configuration sections and future tree integrations

The configuration screen should be divided into focused tabs or pages instead of continuing to grow vertically. Planned sections should separate at least:

- World geometry and capacity
- Island/player defaults
- Tree catalog and enabled trees
- Structural progression such as End portal initial eyes

The tree section should be designed for data-driven entries supplied by other mods or datapacks. It must remain usable when biome mods add enough tree definitions to require pagination, filtering or grouping.

### World geometry remains editable after first island assignment

After the first real island was assigned and persisted as slot 24, Mod Menu correctly disabled the initialized End portal eye control but left capacity and outer radius enabled. The screen also incorrectly continued to state that geometry was editable until the first island.

The test changed the outer radius from 1024 chunks to 128 chunks and saved it. The runtime log then applied `radio_hub=2048`, `radio_interior=1024` and still reported `geometría_bloqueada=false`. Tree defaults were also reduced from 11 to 7 as part of the UI test. The established island coordinates remained persisted, but future allocation in the same world could now use incompatible geometry.

After a full world restart, the altered 128-chunk radius and seven enabled trees were loaded again, capacity/radius remained enabled in Mod Menu, and the server again reported `geometría_bloqueada=false`. Slot 24 and its original OWN/ACTIVE coordinates remained intact. This confirms a persistent world-geometry integrity defect rather than a presentation-only problem.

Expected follow-up:

- Persist and recognize the geometry lock immediately after the first successful island assignment.
- Disable both capacity and radius controls for an established world.
- Display the active persisted world values rather than mutable global defaults.
- Reject geometry changes server-side even if a stale or incompatible client attempts to save them.
- Keep tree availability/default changes separate from structural geometry and never relocate existing islands.

Correction retest:

- The inconsistent test world recovered its original 24-island geometry from the established slot 24 OWN coordinates.
- Runtime now reports `radio_hub=16384`, `radio_interior=8192`, `capacidad=24` and `geometría_bloqueada=true`.
- Mod Menu displays `24 islas` and `Radio exterior: 1024 chunks`; both structural controls are disabled.
- The responsive tab layout no longer overlaps the minimum-difficulty control at the tested automatic GUI scales.

### Selection-state survival protection

Before tree and difficulty selection is complete, a player can remain in the Last Refuge for an arbitrary amount of time. That temporary state should prevent starvation and drowning so exploration or an open menu cannot kill the player.

Expected follow-up:

- Apply protection only while the player is legitimately awaiting tree or difficulty selection in the Nexus.
- Remove it immediately after island assignment or reset completion.
- Do not grant permanent survival effects to established players visiting protected regions through debugging or future progression.

Correction retest:

- A player awaiting selection in the Last Refuge did not lose hunger or drown.
- The temporary protection remained active throughout the selection flow and was suitable for an indefinitely open menu.

### Denied placement consumes the held item client-side

In Survival inside the Nexus, attempted block placement is rejected and the placed block disappears, but the corresponding item is also removed from the player's inventory instead of being restored or resynchronized.

Expected follow-up:

- Cancel destructive placement before inventory consumption whenever the current API permits it.
- Explicitly resynchronize the held slot/inventory after denial if client prediction still decrements the stack.
- Cover ordinary blocks, TNT, fire-starting items and filled buckets without duplicating items.
- Add a regression check that one denied attempt leaves both world state and stack count unchanged.

Correction retest:

- Denied block/item use leaves the world and held inventory unchanged.
- Holding stone while interacting does not place or consume it inside the Nexus.

### Held destructive items suppress otherwise safe interactions

A button placed beside TNT in Creative did not activate that TNT when pressed while a destructive item was held. Repeating the interaction with an empty hand successfully primed the TNT. Existing non-destructive interactables also work with an empty hand.

Expected follow-up:

- Decide whether safe target interactions should still work while the player holds a placeable/destructive item.
- If allowed, classify the target interaction before rejecting use of the held item so buttons, doors and containers remain convenient without permitting placement.
- Preserve the confirmed behavior that primed TNT inside the Nexus cannot destroy blocks while TNT outside it remains vanilla.

Correction retest:

- Doors, levers and buttons work in Survival both with an empty hand and while holding stone.
- The held block remains unplaced and unconsumed during those safe interactions.

### Selected tree may not provide renewable planting stock

The generated mangrove contained no immediately harvestable mangrove propagule. A player can therefore receive the correct tree but lack a guaranteed way to replant it. Similar auditing is required for trees whose viable growth pattern consumes multiple saplings, especially dark oak, and for special plants such as azalea.

Expected follow-up after the test pass:

- Classify every built-in tree by its minimum viable replanting item and quantity.
- Guarantee one recoverable planting set without depending on random leaf drops.
- Let data-driven tree definitions declare their own planting-stock requirement.
- Preserve meaningful difficulty differences; the guarantee should prevent irreversible resource loss rather than become a general resource bonus.

Correction retest:

- Selecting Mangrove placed exactly one mangrove propagule in the final starter-chest slot.
- The guaranteed propagule planted and grew successfully.
- The planting-stock policy was narrowed after testing: Oak, Birch, Spruce, Acacia, Jungle, Cherry and both Azaleas rely on their natural single-item recovery and receive no redundant chest item. Mangrove retains one propagule; Dark Oak and Pale Oak retain four saplings.
- Runtime screenshots confirmed that Cherry and the other ordinary single-propagation trees receive no redundant chest item, while Dark Oak still receives its required set of four saplings.

### Fire spread is not regionally protected

Player fire placement is rejected, but existing fire can spread and destroy combustible blocks inside the Nexus. This includes fire started through the Creative development bypass and potentially fire introduced by future mechanics or entities.

Expected follow-up:

- Cancel fire spread and fire-driven block destruction only inside the logical Nexus region.
- Preserve normal fire behavior outside the Nexus.
- Avoid globally disabling fire visuals or entity damage unless explicitly required.

Correction retest:

- Fire no longer spreads or burns neighboring blocks inside the Nexus.
- Survival players can manually extinguish existing fire without gaining permission to break protected structural blocks.

### Pistons can alter protected blocks

Pistons placed through the Creative bypass remain operable in Survival and can move blocks within the protected Nexus region.

Expected follow-up:

- Prevent pushes and pulls when either the moved block, piston destination or destructive boundary crossing touches the Nexus.
- Preserve normal piston behavior outside the Nexus.
- Cover sticky pistons, slime/honey movement and movement across the circular protection boundary.

Correction retest:

- A piston powered by a lever inside the Nexus no longer activates or moves its target block in Survival.
- The controlling lever still changes position normally, preserving safe interaction feedback while piston movement remains blocked.

### Infernal Wither Skeletons spawn without weapons

Wither Skeletons observed during Infernal Trial VII appeared with empty hands instead of their expected melee weapon. This makes them look like ordinary unarmed skeleton variants and reduces their intended combat role. The current summon functions do not define `HandItems`, so their equipment is not guaranteed explicitly.

Expected follow-up:

- Give trial Wither Skeletons an explicit stone sword, or equipment defined for their trial tier.
- Verify the weapon appears in both phases and at every trial level that can summon this enemy.
- Decide explicitly whether equipped weapons may drop; do not inherit an unintended vanilla drop chance.
- Keep enemy equipment configurable if infernal trial definitions become data-driven later.

Correction retest:

- Trial Wither Skeletons now appear with their intended stone swords.

### Infernal Piglins and champion lack expected equipment and identity

All Piglins and Piglin Brutes observed during Infernal Trial X spawned without weapons or armor. Direct `/summon` commands do not reproduce every natural-spawn initialization step, and the trial currently supplies no explicit equipment.

The final champion sequence did execute: the log recorded the warning at 22:25:03, phase 2 began at 22:25:11 and the encounter completed normally. However, the champion was effectively indistinguishable from the other Piglin Brutes. Its summon definition provides only champion/enemy tags, persistence and zombification immunity; it does not assign a visible custom name, increased scale, equipment or persistent champion-specific combat attributes.

Expected follow-up:

- Give regular Piglins and Piglin Brutes equipment consistent with their vanilla combat roles, with intentional variation where appropriate.
- Ensure ordinary Piglins can receive their expected melee or ranged equipment and Brutes receive golden axes.
- Define armor and equipment drop chances explicitly so trial mobs cannot become an unintended equipment farm.
- Give the final champion a visible localized name, clearly increased size and distinctive equipment/effects.
- Spawn or present the champion in a way that cannot be mistaken for another member of the simultaneous second wave.
- Verify that champion death, rather than an indistinguishable regular enemy, is correctly represented in the encounter flow.

Correction retest:

- Regular Piglins and Piglin Brutes appeared armed, including the expected golden combat equipment and ranged variation.
- Killing phase 1 advanced normally through the eight-second champion presentation into phase 2.
- The final champion was immediately distinguishable by its increased scale, visible localized name, netherite axe and netherite helmet.
- The log identified the named champion's death explicitly and the encounter completed with the personal reward/reliquary flow intact.
